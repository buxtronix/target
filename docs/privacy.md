# Target privacy policy

*Last updated: 24 November 2026*

Target is a word game for Android, made by Ben Buxton. This policy explains
what the app does with information about you. In short: nothing leaves your
phone except the words you choose to look up, and a puzzle download if you
switch on the SMH puzzle.

## What Target collects

Nothing. Target has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no crash
reporting of its own. It doesn't ask for your name, email, location,
contacts or any other personal information.

## What stays on your phone

Your games, settings, stats, streaks and trophies are stored only on your
phone, in the app's private storage. They are deleted if you uninstall the
app or clear its data. Android may include them in your phone's own backup
(to your Google account), under your control and Google's terms.

## When Target goes online

Target works offline. It connects to the internet only when you ask it to:

- **Looking up a definition.** When you tap a word and choose "Look up", the
  word is sent to the dictionary server at dict.org to fetch its meaning.
  Only the word is sent. If you then choose to search the web for it, Target
  opens your browser at a Google search for that word.
- **The SMH Target Time puzzle** (off unless you switch it on in Options).
  When you open one, the puzzle page is downloaded from smh.com.au, the
  Sydney Morning Herald's site. Target isn't affiliated with the SMH; its
  own privacy policy covers that request.

These servers see your phone's internet address, as with any web request.
Target doesn't send them anything else about you.

## Sharing

If you use "Share result", Target hands your result text to the app you
choose. Nothing is shared otherwise.

## Children

Target is suitable for all ages and collects no information from anyone,
including children.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
